A note on nakulī or vāgrasa mantra. The earliest attestation of this mantra is in aitareya āraṇyaka 3.2.5. This version runs as:

> oṣṭhāpidhānā na kulī  
> dantaiḥ parivṛtā paviḥ|  
sarvasyai vāca iśānā   
> cāru māmiha vādayet||   
> Image

The deity is vāc. The entire chapter is a meditation on categories of letters, sambandhas between them and cosmic/biological phenomena. The āraṇyaka calls this as vāgrasa and is deployed when one wishes to be a great speaker in an assembly.

The commentator says "upamārthaḥ na-kāraḥ". I.e. he parses na and kulīdantaiḥ separately and calls "na" as sees it as a particle of similie.

Now, the same mantra appears in chandogya mantra brāhmaṇa with pāṭha bheda. Not just a minor variant reading but the sense itself changes with a change in devatā from vāc to jihvā. It is used in samāvartana ceremony (a graduation bath of the veda student).

Both gobhila and khādira rubricate this mantra. This version reads as:

> oṣṭhāpidhānā nakulī  
> dantaparimitaḥ paviḥ|
jihve mā vihvalo vācaṃ  
cāru mā'dyeha vādaya||

The tongue is prayed for speech that is not stumbling and pleasant. The graduating student is supposed to touch the principal vital breaths with this mantra. 

This ancient mantra seeps into later tantras and can be found in hayagrīva or devī upāsanās. Lot of folks may know this only as the she-mongoose (nakulI) mantra but this directly comes from shruti. /fin
